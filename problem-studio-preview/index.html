04 / PUBLIC CASES

Problems that arrived
from somewhere else.

Five public examples where the visible failure was real — but the useful explanation sat deeper in the system.

These are brief summaries of published cases. They are here to show the pattern, not to reduce complex events to one cause.

NOKIA

The market leader
that couldn't turn quickly enough.

VISIBLE

Nokia lost its position in mobile phones as smartphones shifted toward software, touchscreens and app ecosystems. In 2014 it completed the sale of substantially all of its Devices & Services business to Microsoft.

UNDERNEATH

Published research based on Nokia insiders describes slow decisions, internal rivalry, fragmented software development and important information being filtered on its way upward. The market changed quickly; the organisation struggled to respond at the same speed.

Sources: INSEAD · Nokia
SAMSUNG GALAXY NOTE7

A flagship launch
that became a recall.

VISIBLE

Reports of Galaxy Note7 battery incidents led Samsung to launch a replacement programme and later end production of the device.

UNDERNEATH

Samsung's investigation, alongside three independent organisations, concluded that the batteries caused the incidents. The visible failure was the phone; the identified cause sat inside a critical component and the checks around it.

Sources: Samsung investigation · Samsung Australia
LEFT-PAD · 2016

Eleven lines
that stopped builds.

VISIBLE

Software projects began failing when the small left-pad package disappeared from the npm registry. npm recorded hundreds of failures per minute and a disruption lasting about two and a half hours.

UNDERNEATH

The package sat inside dependency chains used by thousands of projects. The code was tiny; the dependency was not. Removing one small piece exposed how much of the wider system relied on it.

Sources: npm postmortem · The Register
WIRECARD · 2020

The numbers
nobody could verify.

VISIBLE

Wirecard said in June 2020 that €1.9 billion recorded in trust accounts probably did not exist. Three days later, the company filed for insolvency.

UNDERNEATH

The auditor had been unable to confirm the cash balances, and Wirecard reported evidence of false balance confirmations. The case shows why a number is only as reliable as the controls and independent checks behind it.

Sources: Wirecard statement reporting · Insolvency announcement
AUTOMOTIVE CHIP SHORTAGE

A small component
that stopped assembly lines.

VISIBLE

Semiconductor shortages caused severe vehicle-production disruption in 2021 and 2022, including assembly lines being brought to a standstill.

UNDERNEATH

A missing semiconductor could constrain the output of an entire vehicle plant. More capacity elsewhere could not compensate for the component the finished product still needed.

Sources: U.S. Federal Reserve · U.S. Commerce

Plain-English glossary

Words should help you.
They should make things clearer.

These are the meanings we use on this site. They are deliberately practical, not academic.

If a term could reasonably mean different things to different people, we define what we mean here. Linked terms across the site use a dotted underline and open the matching definition.

0 terms

Bottleneck

A point where work is restricted or delayed because capacity or flow is lower than the demand arriving there.

Capability

The skill, knowledge, tools and conditions needed to perform a type of work effectively.

Capacity

The amount of useful work a person, team or system can realistically handle within a period.

Constraint

The factor that currently limits the outcome or flow of the wider system.

Control

A check or safeguard designed to reduce error, risk or unwanted variation.

Decision rights

Who has authority to make a particular decision, who must be consulted, and when that decision is final.

Demand governance

The rules for what work enters the system, who can request it, how it is prioritised, and who can override that order.

Dependency

Something a task, team or system relies on before it can proceed or work correctly.

Diagnostic

A structured investigation used to understand what is happening and what may be causing it before choosing a response.

Escalation

Moving a problem or decision to someone with greater authority because it cannot be resolved where it sits.

Evidence

Information that can support, weaken or disprove an explanation. Evidence is stronger when it can be independently checked.

Exception

A case that is allowed to bypass the usual rule, process or standard.

Feedback loop

A cycle where the result of an action is fed back into future decisions or behaviour.

Flow

How smoothly work moves through a system from start to finish, including where it waits, loops or gets interrupted.

Handoff

The point where work, information or responsibility moves from one person, team or system to another.

Hypothesis

A possible explanation that must be tested against evidence before it is treated as true.

Incentive

A reward, pressure or consequence that makes one behaviour more rational or attractive than another.

Intervention

A deliberate change made to try to alter an outcome.

Lens

A perspective used to examine one part of a problem without assuming that part is the cause.

Mechanism

The process or behaviour that could be producing the result you can see.

Metric

A measure used to track performance, activity or an outcome. A metric is only useful if it represents the thing you actually care about.

Operating model

How an organisation actually gets work done: roles, decisions, workflows, information, incentives and routines.

Outcome

The result that matters after the work is done, rather than the activity used to get there.

Pattern

A repeated shape in events, behaviour or evidence. A pattern is a clue, not proof of a cause.

Process

A repeatable set of activities used to produce an outcome.

Queue

Work that is waiting for attention, a decision, information or capacity before it can continue.

Rework

Work that has to be repeated, corrected or rebuilt because the first pass did not produce a usable result.

Root cause

The underlying condition or combination of conditions that produces a recurring problem.

Sample

A subset of people, cases, events or data examined to learn about a wider situation.

Signal

An observation that may indicate a meaningful change or pattern. A signal is something to investigate, not proof by itself.

Symptom

The visible or experienced problem. The symptom may be real even when its cause sits somewhere else.

System

A set of connected people, processes, tools, decisions and incentives whose interactions produce an overall result.

System-level

Looking at the behaviour of the whole connected system rather than one team or component in isolation.

Trade-off

A choice where improving one thing may require giving up or accepting less of something else.

Triangulation

Checking the same question against multiple independent sources or types of evidence.

Workflow

The real sequence of steps through which work moves from request to completion.

Workaround

An informal way of getting work done when the intended process or system does not work well enough.

Work in progress

Work that has started but is not yet finished. Too much at once can create queues, switching and delay.
